Game concept pitch

Numception working title

Find the sum hidden in a shape, and your answer becomes one number in a bigger puzzle of the same shape. Then zoom out and keep going.

  • Numbers sit in a shape. Tap the three that make a op b = c.
  • When you solve it, the piece changes from unsolved to solved, and c becomes a cell of the next puzzle.
  • The inception zoom: the camera pulls out until the finished puzzle is a single cell, and the bigger puzzle builds itself from your answers.

Two moods

Zen Paper and one ink colour. No points: each solve adds a light to a lifetime constellation. A wrong tap sinks like a pebble, errors are never counted, and difficulty quietly eases.
Normal Black with one neon. Speed points and a combo shown as glow. A wrong tap is a power cut that kills the combo but costs no points. Slow and steady beats fast and sloppy.

The long game: an endless pyramid

Puzzles stack into an endless tree. Home is that tree: zoom out to see how big it is, then glide to the next unsolved puzzle. Solving pushes territory out of the fog, and lifetime progress reads as altitude, from the Eiffel Tower to Everest to the Moon.

Tower rounds are the short version: a bounded 3-tier mini-pyramid (9 + 3 + 1 = 13 puzzles at n = 3) for one session.

What the POC proves

  • A classic round and a tower round, with the zoom and solve transition at the centre.
  • Both Zen and Normal.
  • A +/− puzzle generator that checks each puzzle has one answer.
  • Rating logs that will drive adaptive difficulty and surprise-at-mastery.

Why it's different

  • The core is one moment. The solve transition and the zoom work the same at every scale, so a puzzle deep in the pyramid feels as good as the first.
  • Surprise at mastery, no unlock tree. When you settle into a groove, a new operator turns up unannounced, with a glyph you haven't seen, and its first use is easy (3² = 9). Finding it is the unlock.
  • One-time $2–5. No ads, no pay-to-win.
  • Minimal lines and shapes. The geometry is the HUD. Numbers are the only text. Closer to Monument Valley than to Candy Crush.

Why this wins

Players' biggest complaints in this category are about monetization. They object to membership upsells, ads every round, and math that gates the game instead of being the game. Numception's math is the game, and the game is a one-time purchase. (Retention research)

Leaderboards and expected rewards backfire for anxious kids. Maths-anxious children disliked a leaderboard whatever their rank, which is why Zen has no points at all. (Retention research)

Platforms

Web-first in TypeScript, wrapped with Capacitor for iOS and Android. The generator, difficulty model and scoring live in an engine-agnostic package.recommended, pending prototype

A 1–2 day glow and particle prototype on a mid-range Android phone and an older iPhone will settle the renderer before the architecture is locked.
