# domain research: Name and trademark

**Decision this research serves:** Whether Kakooma is usable and what direction the new name should take

_This is not legal advice. The trademark databases could not be queried directly this run; see Open questions._

## Executive summary

**Do not use "Kakooma", or anything confusingly close to it.** Rename now, with an *invented* word you can protect, and keep the puzzle mechanic, which is free to use.

What drives the answer:

1. **Kakooma is an active brand owned by Greg Tang / Tang Math.**
   - Greg Tang created it, and it existed by 2011 [1].
   - Tang Math still sells Kakooma® online variants, physical card games (its own store and Amazon) and an iOS app under that name [2][3][4].
   - The owner marks the name ® throughout. In the US, ® may only be used on a federally registered mark, so a registration almost certainly exists, although the registration number was not confirmed [3][4].
2. **The mechanic itself is not protectable by copyright.**
   - The US Copyright Office says copyright does not cover a game's idea, name or method of play [5].
   - US copyright law (17 U.S.C. §102(b)) excludes functional rules and mechanics. Protection covers the art, text and audiovisual presentation [6].
   - A "find a op b = c among the numbers" game is fair ground, provided our art, text and name are our own. A third-party "Kakooma: Math Brain Puzzles" iOS app appeared in 2025 [7], but it borrowed the *name*, which is exactly the risk to avoid.
3. **KenKen is the precedent.**
   - Nextoy holds the KenKen and KenDoku trademarks. Rivals publish the identical puzzle as "Calcudoku" or "Mathdoku" [8][9].
   - Descriptive names ("Mathdoku", "-le" spin-offs like Nerdle) end up crowded [8][10]. That they are therefore weak trademarks is our inference; the sources do not say it.
   - The protectable names in this space are invented coinages (KenKen; Sudoku in Japan) [8][11]. That pattern is inferred across sources rather than stated by one.

**Biggest caveat:** a candidate name is not "available" until it clears a USPTO search in Nice classes 9, 28 and 41 plus common-law use [12][13], an App Store and Google Play name check [14][15], and a domain check. Budget an attorney clearance search before spending money on branding.

## Rules of the game: what the name must survive

- **USPTO clearance.** The USPTO says to search for marks "confusingly similar" to yours on related goods and services, and to check the Official Gazette and common-law use, including state registries [12].
- **Classes to clear.** A game brand typically spans Nice class 9 (game software), 28 (games, including card games) and 41 (online entertainment) [13]. This comes from a secondary source; USPTO's own guidance names no classes. Tang Math's physical card games make class 28 directly relevant to any "-ooma" sound-alike.
- **Apple.**
  - App names are capped at 30 characters and must be unique [14].
  - You may not make "minor changes to another app's name" or use another developer's brand without approval [14].
  - Search results truncate at roughly 26 characters, so the distinctive word should come first (low-medium confidence) [16].
- **Google Play.** The title is capped at 30 characters, with no ranking or price claims and no emoji [15].

## The landscape: how math-puzzle names work

| Family | Examples | Protectability |
|---|---|---|
| Invented, Japanese-feel coinage | KenKen, Sudoku, Kakuro, Kakooma | Strong when owned (KenKen [8], Sudoku in Japan [11]) |
| Generic "-doku" mash-ups | Calcudoku, Mathdoku, KenDoku | Crowded; they exist precisely to route around trademarks [8][9] |
| "-le" after Wordle | Nerdle, Primel | Trendy in 2022, now dated and crowded [10] |

## Recommendations

1. **Rename (high confidence).** Drop "Kakooma" everywhere. Done 2026-10-08: the project, repo and docs now use the working title **Numception**. Feeds: product brief and PRD title.
2. **Naming direction for the brainstorm (medium confidence, partly inferred):**
   - Invent a coined word, two to three syllables, distinct in sound from Kakooma, KenKen and "-doku" names.
   - Ideally it should suggest the game's own ideas: nesting and fractals, triangles and geometry, the "a ∘ b = c" triple, or a neon look.
   - Keep the store title within 30 characters, with the coined word first.
3. **Clearance checklist per shortlisted name:**
   1. USPTO tmsearch in classes 9, 28 and 41.
   2. WIPO Global Brand Database.
   3. App Store and Play search.
   4. `.com` / `.app` / `.game` domains.
   5. Steam and itch.io.
   6. Social handles.
   7. An attorney knockout search before launch.

## Open questions

- **KAKOOMA registration details** (number, classes, owner, live or dead). The USPTO and Justia searches failed to load. A two-minute manual search at tmsearch.uspto.gov answers this, but it does not change the recommendation.
- **Whether Tang holds a patent on the format.** No evidence was found. Patents are the only route to protecting a mechanic [6]. Worth a Google Patents check on inventor "Gregory Tang" before launch.
- **Who built the 2025 third-party "Kakooma" app, and whether it was licensed or challenged.** Unknown [7].
